## The check almost nobody plans around

Read through eligibility sections across this site's programme guides and a quiet pattern shows up again and again: alongside the route-specific requirements — funds, a job offer, a sponsor, a business plan — sits a character and health check that shows up almost everywhere, under different names. Canada calls it a police certificate and an Immigration Medical Examination. The UK asks for a tuberculosis test from applicants of certain nationalities. Australia folds it into a broader "health and character requirements" test. Vietnam's work permit route carries its own version. The names differ; the underlying idea — that a government wants to confirm an applicant is not a criminal or public-health risk before granting entry — is close to universal.

What makes this worth its own explainer, rather than a line item buried in each programme page, is that these checks behave differently from most other requirements in one important way: they often have to be arranged from the applicant's home country, sometimes months in advance, and cannot be rushed the way some paperwork can.

## Police certificates: a document with its own lead time

A police certificate — proof of no disqualifying criminal record, generally covering everywhere an applicant has lived for a meaningful period — is not something most national or local police services issue same-day. Processing can take weeks, and for an applicant who has lived in multiple countries, a separate certificate is often needed from each one. This site's Canada guides flag exactly this: Express Entry and spousal sponsorship both list a police certificate among required documents, and the practical lesson is the same in both cases — starting this early, well before the rest of an application is otherwise ready, avoids it becoming the bottleneck that holds up a file that is complete in every other respect.

## Medical exams and TB tests: assessed on where you have lived, not just who you are

Health screening follows a related but distinct logic. Canada's Immigration Medical Examination is a general requirement across its economic and family routes. The UK's approach is narrower and nationality-specific: a tuberculosis test is required only for applicants from certain countries, a list that this site's UK guides repeatedly flag as something to confirm directly rather than assume, since which nationalities are on it can change. Australia bundles this into its broader health requirement assessed alongside character on routes including the partner visa and Skilled Independent visa.

The practical implication is the same across all three: whether a medical or TB check applies at all can depend on where an applicant has lived, not simply which visa they are applying for — which means the answer has to be checked for the specific applicant's history, not assumed from a general read of the route.

## Why this belongs in the plan from day one

The reason this document category deserves its own explainer, rather than being left as a line in each programme's document checklist, is timing risk. Funds can often be assembled relatively quickly once a decision is made. A job offer or sponsor relationship is either in place or it is not. But a police certificate from a previous country of residence, or a medical exam booked through an approved panel physician, can have its own multi-week timeline that runs independently of everything else in the application — and unlike some other documents, it is not always something that can be expedited by paying more or working harder.

## What to actually do about it

Treat the character and health check as one of the very first things to look into for a specific route, not one of the last — identify every country of prior residence that will need its own police certificate, confirm whether a medical or TB exam applies based on the applicant's specific nationality and residence history, and book any required exam through the route's approved provider rather than an unapproved local doctor, since an exam from the wrong provider is often not accepted at all. The individual programme pages on this site list this requirement where it applies; this guide exists because the requirement is easy to under-weight next to more visible, route-specific paperwork.
