## Recognition is a separate process, with its own outcome

Holding a real, legitimate qualification is the starting point for a skills or credential assessment, not a guarantee of how it will be assessed. Two routes on this site record the same underlying mistake, treating that assessment as a formality that confirms what an applicant already knows rather than a genuinely separate process that can produce a different answer.

## A step delayed until it becomes the bottleneck

Australia's Skilled Independent visa depends on a skills assessment specific to the applicant's nominated occupation, carried out by a separate assessing authority, not by the Department of Home Affairs itself. This site's guide records delaying the skills assessment as a pitfall in its own right — because the assessment sits outside the visa application's own timeline, starting it late does not just risk a slower process, it risks the assessment becoming the single item holding up an otherwise-ready application. The same guide separately records under-evidencing points-test claims, a related but distinct risk: even a favourable assessment outcome still has to be supported by evidence that meets the points test's own specific standard.

## A degree that is real and still not automatically equivalent

Germany's EU Blue Card requires a recognised university degree, or in defined cases equivalent professional experience, and this site's guide records leaving degree recognition until late as a named pitfall, separate from the salary threshold the route also requires. A genuine degree from a legitimate institution is not automatically treated as comparable to a German qualification — that comparison is its own determination, and confirming it early is what keeps it from becoming the deciding factor at the last stage of an otherwise strong application.

## What this means before assuming a qualification "counts"

Neither pitfall recorded here is about an applicant's qualification being illegitimate. Both are about treating an external, separate recognition or assessment process as though its outcome were already decided by the underlying qualification itself. Where a route depends on a credential assessment or degree recognition step, the practical lesson is the same in both countries: start that step as early as the route allows, and treat its outcome as genuinely undetermined until it is confirmed, not as a formality standing between the applicant and a result they already expect.
