## The protection is not automatic, and it is not the same everywhere

An expiry date approaching is not, by itself, a status a permit holder is simply left to manage alone — several of the countries this site covers have a mechanism for someone who has applied to extend or change status before their current permission runs out. But how much protection that mechanism gives, and what triggers it, varies sharply, and assuming one country's rule applies to another is a real and recorded risk.

## Canada's maintained status, and the one condition that has to be met first

Canada's system, reported as maintained status (formerly called implied status), is the most generous version recorded here: someone who applies to extend or change their status before their current permit expires can generally continue on the same conditions — including the right to keep working, for a worker — while the application is decided, for as long as they remain in Canada. The entire mechanism depends on one hard condition: the extension application has to reach Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada while the existing status is still in force. An application submitted after the permit has already expired does not activate maintained status, however close to the deadline it was filed.

## Vietnam and the residence card that does not renew itself

Vietnam's routes carry a sharper version of the same risk, because the underlying document does not describe itself as temporary in the way a "permit" does. This site's guide records missing the 10-year renewal on the Permanent Residence Card as a named pitfall — the underlying permanent status does not expire, but the physical card is a separate document that does, and the fact that the status itself is indefinite is precisely what makes it easy to overlook that the card is not. Vietnam's work permit carries the more direct version: this site's guide records treating the permit as permanent as a pitfall in its own right, when it is not.

## The UK's absence rule keeps running even while a status decision waits

The UK's Indefinite Leave to Remain shows a version of this risk that has nothing to do with application timing at all. The 180-day absence rule is assessed on a rolling 12-month basis across the whole qualifying period leading up to a decision, not reset or paused by anything an applicant does in the run-up to applying. Someone approaching their qualifying date with an otherwise strong case can still fail this specific check if their travel pattern breached the rolling limit at any point, regardless of how carefully everything else around the expiry date was managed.

## Working during the gap, and what it does to the record

Where a genuine gap-filling mechanism exists, working within its terms is not itself the problem — Canada's maintained status explicitly preserves a worker's existing right to work while a decision is pending. The risk is narrower and sharper: working under the assumption that a protection exists, when the application was filed too late to trigger it, or in a country where no equivalent mechanism was confirmed for that specific route. That gap, between assumed protection and actual protection, is where working during an expiry window turns into working without authorisation, a materially different problem than a late application on its own.

## What actually happens if someone leaves during that window

Leaving the country during a pending extension decision is its own separate risk, distinct from working during it, and the two should not be assumed to carry the same consequence. Whether an application already filed survives a departure, and whether re-entry is possible while it remains pending, depends on the specific route and country and is not something this page states as a general rule — it is exactly the kind of detail that needs to be confirmed against the current official guidance for that specific application before a decision to travel is made, not assumed either way.

## What this means before a permit's expiry date arrives

Where a genuine extension mechanism exists, filing before the current permit expires — not close to it, before it — is what activates it; a same-day or after-the-fact filing generally does not qualify for the same protection. Where the underlying status is indefinite but tied to a renewable card, the renewal deadline needs its own separate tracking, since the status not expiring is exactly what makes the card's own expiry easy to miss. And any assumption about working rights or re-entry rights during a pending decision needs to be checked against the current rule for that specific route, not carried over from a different country's system.
