## The visa fee is the smallest number on the page

Four study routes on this site record a version of the same pitfall: underestimating the true cost of the route, not because the visa fee is hidden, but because the real cost sits in places a first read of the fee schedule does not naturally look. Read the four together and the pattern is consistent — the cost that catches people out is charged per year rather than once, held rather than spent, or owed to a party other than the government.

## Charged per year, not as a single figure

The UK's Student visa carries the clearest example: the Immigration Health Surcharge is charged per year of the course, not as a flat one-off amount. A three or four-year degree multiplies the surcharge accordingly, and this site's guide records underestimating this on a multi-year course as its own distinct pitfall, separate from the headline visa fee. A budget built around the application fee alone can be wrong by a large multiple once the surcharge is properly annualised across the course length.

## Money that has to be held, not spent

Two routes turn the cost question into a timing problem rather than a simple total. Germany's student route requires a blocked account, and this site's guide records underestimating the setup time for that account as a pitfall distinct from the amount itself — the account has to be established and funded before the visa process can properly proceed, and leaving that until late compresses a timeline that has its own lead time. The same guide flags a related trap: assuming a scholarship always removes the need for a blocked account, when in fact the specific scholarship terms determine that, not the fact of holding one at all.

Canada's study permit route carries the same shape from a different angle: the guide records budgeting to last year's financial threshold as a pitfall, because the required amount is a published figure that moves, and a budget built against an outdated number can fall short of the current requirement without anyone having done anything obviously wrong.

## The cost that is not the government's at all

Australia's Student visa guide records underestimating the true cost as a named pitfall in its own right, distinct from any single line item — tuition, health cover and living cost evidence combine into a total that the headline visa application fee alone gives no real sense of. This is the same lesson the other three routes demonstrate from different angles, stated plainly: the visa fee tells an applicant almost nothing about what the route actually costs to complete.

## What this means for planning a budget

Treat the published visa application fee as the floor, not the estimate. For any of these four routes, check separately: whether a health surcharge or equivalent is charged per year rather than once, whether funds have to be held in a specific account rather than simply available, whether a published financial threshold has moved since it was last checked, and what the full cost of tuition and living evidence actually totals once combined. Each of these is a real, separately documented cost on this site's own programme pages — the pitfall is not that the numbers are hidden, but that a first read of the fee schedule alone will not surface all of them.
