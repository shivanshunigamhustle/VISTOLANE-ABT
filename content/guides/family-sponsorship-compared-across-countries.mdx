## Five routes, five different gates

Family sponsorship sounds like it should be the same idea everywhere: a citizen or resident sponsors their spouse or partner, and the couple is assessed on whether the relationship is genuine. That part is true across every route on this site. What differs sharply is what else has to be proven before the relationship even gets assessed — and that second gate is where an applicant's actual experience of each country diverges most.

The United Kingdom's spouse and partner visa is built around a strict financial threshold for the sponsor — recently reported at £29,000 a year from eligible income, or £88,500 in savings, checked against defined evidence categories rather than a general impression of the couple's finances. Germany's route asks something different of the applicant, not the sponsor: basic German language ability at level A1, on top of proof of suitable accommodation and sufficient financial resources. Australia's partner visa carries neither a language nor an income test as its primary gate, but does carry one of the highest one-off government charges of any family route this site has documented — a combined application charge reported at AUD 11,710 for the primary applicant alone.

## Where the real friction sits, route by route

The pattern worth noticing is that each country has picked a different place to put the difficulty. The UK's is financial and falls on the sponsor. Germany's is linguistic and falls on the applicant. Australia's is a straightforward, large fee that falls on whoever is paying for the application. Vietnam's TT visa is structurally different again — it does not center on a financial or language test at all, but on the Vietnamese sponsor actively initiating and carrying a sponsorship filing with the immigration authority, meaning the process cannot proceed on the foreign partner's initiative alone. Canada's spousal sponsorship route, by contrast, is one where this site's own research was unable to confirm exact current fee figures with confidence — several fee line items in Canada's guide are marked as needing verification rather than stated as settled, which is itself a reminder that "no confirmed number" is not the same as "no cost."

This matters for planning in a very practical way. A couple assuming every family route works like the one they have already read about — say, assuming every country wants proof of income the way the UK does — can badly misjudge what a different country's route will actually ask of them. Germany's A1 language requirement, for instance, needs active preparation time that a couple focused only on financial documentation could easily under-plan for.

## The relationship test is the one constant

Underneath these differences, every route on this site asks the same underlying question: is this a genuine, subsisting relationship, not one entered into primarily to secure immigration status. The UK and Australia both draw an explicit line for unmarried partners — the UK generally requiring at least 2 years in a relationship resembling marriage before it is assessed on the same basis, Australia distinguishing stages of its partner visa process around genuineness and continuity. That shared foundation is worth remembering when the differences above start to feel arbitrary: the variation is in what a country demands to see proof of first, not in whether the relationship itself has to be real.

## What this means in practice

Before assuming a family sponsorship route will unfold the way a friend's did in a different country, check three things specifically for the country in question: what the sponsor has to prove (income, savings, or simply eligibility to sponsor), what the applicant has to prove (language, health, character), and what fees are actually confirmed versus still needing verification. The individual programme pages on this site are built to make that distinction visible — a "Verify before publish" marker on a fee or requirement means exactly what it says, and should be checked against the current official source before it shapes a specific couple's plan.
