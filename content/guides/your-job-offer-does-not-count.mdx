## A genuine offer and a qualifying offer are different tests

An employer wanting to hire someone, and being willing to put that in writing, is not the same as the offer satisfying a specific route's own technical definition of what counts. This gap catches genuinely committed employers and genuinely qualified applicants more often than a bad-faith offer does, because the requirement being missed is usually procedural, not about the job itself.

## The salary threshold is not one number

The UK's Skilled Worker visa is the clearest case: the salary threshold is not a single published figure but an occupation-specific "going rate," and this site's guide records assuming any salary above the general headline threshold qualifies as its own named pitfall. An offer can clear the widely-quoted general figure and still fall short of the specific rate set for that applicant's actual occupation code — the real bar moves with the role, and checking against the wrong number produces an offer that looks sufficient and is not.

## The salary is right, and the degree is the problem

Germany's EU Blue Card shows the same shape from a different angle. The route requires both a salary above its threshold and a university degree recognised as comparable to a German qualification — meeting the salary while leaving degree recognition unconfirmed is recorded as its own distinct pitfall on this site's guide. A well-paid, genuine offer does not itself resolve the separate, independent question of whether the applicant's qualification is recognised, and the two failures look identical from the outside: a Blue Card application that does not proceed.

## The offer exists, and the occupation is not on the list

Australia's Skills in Demand visa and similar routes narrow the test a third way: not any job offer qualifies, only one in an eligible occupation, and this site's guide records assuming otherwise as a pitfall in its own right. An employer can make a completely genuine offer in a role the business urgently needs filled, and the offer still will not qualify the visa if that specific occupation does not sit on the route's own eligible list.

## The offer is real, and the employer is the one not ready

Canada's LMIA route adds a fourth failure mode that has nothing to do with the offer's terms at all: before an employer can even file, they must complete a defined recruitment process, and this site's guide records advertising that falls short of the requirement as a named pitfall. The job offer itself can be entirely genuine while the employer's own compliance step is incomplete, and the LMIA cannot proceed until it is — a problem the worker did not create and cannot fix directly.

## What to check before assuming an offer qualifies

Before treating a job offer as sufficient for a specific work route, four things need checking against that route's own current rules, not against a general impression of what "a good job offer" should satisfy: whether the salary clears the role-specific threshold, not just the headline figure; whether any required qualification has been formally recognised, separately from the salary question; whether the specific occupation sits on the route's current eligible list; and whether the employer's own procedural obligations, where the route has them, are actually complete. A genuine offer failing on any one of these reads, to the route itself, exactly the same as an offer that never existed.
