## A document that expires on its own schedule, not the application's

A medical exam or a police certificate is not filed once and forgotten — each carries its own validity window, running from the date it was completed, independent of how long the surrounding application takes to decide. When processing runs longer than expected, which this site's own guide on reading processing times treats as the norm rather than the exception, a certificate that was current when submitted can lapse before a decision is reached.

## What is actually confirmed, and what still needs checking for a specific case

For Canada, an Immigration Medical Examination is reported valid for 12 months from the date of the exam, with that clock starting on the appointment date, not the date the results reach the immigration authority. Police certificates are reported less standardised: typically valid for somewhere between 6 and 12 months, with the exact figure depending on the issuing country rather than a single fixed rule. Verify before publish: confirm the current specific validity periods for both document types, and any country-specific variation in police certificate validity, directly against the current official guidance before relying on either figure for a specific application, since these periods are exactly the kind of operational detail that changes without much notice.

## Why this risk is structural, not a rare mistake

Two features of the process make this a recurring risk rather than an unlucky exception. First, these documents are often gathered early, before the rest of an application is complete, precisely because police certificates in particular can themselves take weeks to obtain — the same early start that avoids one timing problem creates exposure to this one. Second, overall processing times are, by this site's own reporting elsewhere, rolling figures that move with volume and are not guarantees for any individual file; a case that runs longer than the published estimate is not unusual, and a certificate timed against the published estimate rather than a more conservative buffer is exposed to exactly that overrun.

## What to actually do if a certificate is close to expiring

Track each certificate's own expiry date separately from the application's overall timeline, not as a single combined deadline. Where a decision is genuinely close but a certificate is due to lapse first, contact the relevant authority before it expires rather than after, since the options available before an expiry and after one are typically different. And where an application's processing time is running long enough that a lapse looks likely, treat renewing the certificate proactively as cheaper, in time and certainty, than waiting to find out whether the expired version will be accepted.
