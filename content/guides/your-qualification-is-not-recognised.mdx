## The qualification is not what is being assessed. The recognition is.

A skills or credential assessment does not ask whether a qualification is genuine — a genuine qualification is assumed. It asks whether that qualification is comparable to a defined local standard, assessed by a specific body under specific criteria, and the answer to that second question is not decided by the qualification itself. This is why a real, legitimately earned degree can still come back from an assessment as not recognised, or recognised at a lower level than expected.

## Started late, and the timeline became the problem

Australia's Skilled Independent visa depends on a skills assessment carried out by a separate assessing authority specific to the applicant's nominated occupation, not by the immigration department itself. This site's guide records delaying the skills assessment as a named pitfall, and the reason it matters is structural: the assessment sits entirely outside the visa application's own timeline, so starting it late does not just slow things down, it risks the assessment becoming the single item holding up an otherwise-ready application, discovered only once everything else is prepared.

## Recognised, and still not enough on its own

Germany's EU Blue Card shows a version of the same risk with an added layer. The route requires a degree recognised as comparable to a German qualification, and this site's guide records leaving that recognition until late as a pitfall separate from the salary threshold the route also requires. Recognition confirmed does not finish the case either — it is one of two independent conditions, and a strong salary offer does not compensate for an unresolved recognition question, any more than confirmed recognition compensates for a salary that falls short.

## An accurate assessment result, and still not enough evidence

Even a favourable outcome is not automatically the end of the risk. Australia's Skilled Independent guide separately records under-evidencing points-test claims — a genuinely accurate assessment result still has to be supported by documentation that meets the points test's own specific evidentiary standard, not simply cited as a fact. An applicant can hold a correct, favourable assessment and still fail to claim the points it should support, if the surrounding paperwork does not meet the standard the points test itself expects.

## What to actually do before assuming a qualification "counts"

Treat credential recognition and skills assessment as their own project, running in parallel with the rest of an application, not as a formality confirmed by the fact that the underlying degree is real. Start the process as early as the specific route allows — before other evidence is gathered, not after. Where a route depends on both recognition and a separate threshold, such as a salary, treat them as two conditions that both have to independently clear, not one that implies the other. And where a favourable outcome depends on being properly evidenced, such as a points claim, confirm the assessment result is documented to the standard the next stage actually requires, not just held as a certificate on file.
