## Enough money is not the same as proven money

Three routes on this site turn a straightforward-sounding requirement — show you can support yourself — into one of the more mechanical, least forgiving tests any of them run. In each case, the underlying finances can be genuinely sufficient and the application can still fail, because "proof of funds" is assessed against a specific structure, not a general financial picture.

## A holding period that resets on a single bad day

The UK's Student visa runs the strictest version recorded on this site: the required maintenance funds have to be held for a full consecutive 28 days before applying, and if the balance drops below the threshold on even one day inside that window, the clock resets from the beginning. This site's guide records breaking the 28-day maintenance rule as its own pitfall, and it is described there as one of the most common reasons an otherwise straightforward application is refused on what amounts to a technicality — a fund transfer timed too close to the application date, or an account that dipped briefly for an unrelated reason, can undo weeks of preparation.

## An account that has to exist in a specific form

Germany's student route does not merely ask for a sufficient balance — it requires the money to sit in a blocked account, a specific financial product with its own setup process. This site's guide records underestimating the setup time for that account as a pitfall distinct from the amount itself, since the account has to be established and funded before the visa process can properly proceed. The same guide flags a second, related trap: assuming a scholarship automatically removes the need for a blocked account, when the specific terms of the scholarship, not the fact of holding one, determine whether it does.

## A threshold that is a moving target

Canada's study permit route shows a third failure mode: the required financial threshold is a published figure that changes, and this site's guide records budgeting to last year's figure as its own pitfall. Funds that were genuinely sufficient against an older published number can fall short of the current requirement without any change in the applicant's actual financial position — only in the figure they were checked against.

## What "proof of funds" actually requires

Across all three routes, the common thread is that a comfortable financial position is necessary but not sufficient. What clears the requirement is the specific structure each route defines: the right holding period, in the right account type, checked against the current published threshold rather than whatever figure was quoted when planning began. Confirming these three things directly against the current official source, well before the funds are needed, is the only way any of these three tests behave as forgivingly as the underlying finances actually are.
