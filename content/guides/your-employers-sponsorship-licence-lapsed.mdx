## An employer's compliance problem that becomes a worker's immigration problem

A sponsored worker's status depends on their employer holding a valid sponsor licence, which means an employer's own compliance failure — something entirely outside the worker's control or knowledge — can put the worker's status at risk. Suspension and revocation are reported as materially different events, and confusing the two leads to either unnecessary panic or a dangerously casual response.

## Suspension: frozen, not lost

When a sponsor licence is suspended, the employer's ability to sponsor new workers is reported frozen while the Home Office investigates a suspected breach of sponsor duties — but the licence itself remains technically valid during that period. Existing sponsored workers are reported generally able to continue working for the employer in the role stated on their Certificate of Sponsorship throughout a suspension. What stops is new activity: the employer cannot assign new Certificates of Sponsorship while suspended, which matters for a worker mid-application or hoping to change role internally, but does not by itself end an existing worker's current employment authorisation.

## Revocation: a hard deadline starts immediately

Revocation is reported as substantially more serious. The consequences include loss of the employer's sponsored-worker employment rights altogether, immediate curtailment of affected workers' visas, and no right of appeal against the revocation decision itself. Workers already sponsored are reported generally retaining their visa in the short term but cannot be re-sponsored by that same employer, and are reported having around 60 days from the revocation to find a new sponsor before facing immigration consequences. This is not a slow-moving administrative process — the clock is reported starting immediately on revocation, not on some later formal notice.

## Why this is not a rare or hypothetical risk

This happens at real scale. Reported figures for the first quarter of 2026 show 1,712 sponsor licences suspended and 1,545 revoked — thousands of employers, and by extension thousands of sponsored workers, affected inside a single three-month period. A worker who has never had any personal compliance issue can still be caught by this, purely because their employer's own sponsor duties were not met.

## What to actually do if this happens

First, establish which of the two events has actually occurred — suspension and revocation carry very different timelines and very different levels of urgency, and treating a suspension with revocation-level panic, or a revocation with suspension-level calm, both lead to the wrong response. Where the employer's licence has been revoked, the roughly 60-day window to secure a new sponsor is the number to plan around immediately, not a nominal figure to keep in mind. And because a re-application by the same employer after revocation is reported barred for around 12 months, a worker whose employer is revoked should not expect that employer to become a viable sponsor again on any timeline relevant to their own status.
