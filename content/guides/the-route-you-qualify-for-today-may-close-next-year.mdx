## A route is a snapshot, not a promise

Every programme guide on this site carries a "last reviewed" date for a reason beyond bookkeeping: the rules underneath it move, sometimes substantially, and an applicant working from outdated guidance is not making a small mistake — they are planning around a route that may no longer exist in the form they read about. This is not a hypothetical risk. It has already happened, twice, among the routes this site covers.

## When the whole route changed shape

Australia's former Business Innovation and Investment programme, including its subclass 188 and 132 investment streams, closed to new applications on 31 July 2024. It was replaced by the National Innovation Visa, an achievement-based route that assesses an applicant's track record rather than the size of their capital — not a rebrand of the old programme, but a different test entirely. This site's guide records relying on outdated guidance about the old investor programme as its own pitfall, and it is easy to see why: an article or adviser describing Australia's investor route in capital terms, written before mid-2024, is not describing a route that still exists.

The UK's Indefinite Leave to Remain may be next. A government consultation on lengthening the general qualifying period from 5 years to 10 closed in February 2026, and while the change has been reported as expected around autumn 2026, it had not taken effect as of this site's last review. This site's guide records assuming the 5-year figure applies to every route as a pitfall for a related reason — several routes already qualify sooner or later than the general figure — but the pending consultation adds a second, time-sensitive version of the same risk: the general figure itself may not hold much longer.

## When the rules inside a route kept moving

Two further routes show the same instability at a smaller scale, inside a programme that has not closed but keeps being adjusted. Canada's Express Entry guide records planning around a ranking system that is under review as a pitfall — the Comprehensive Ranking System's inputs are not fixed, and a plan built entirely around today's scoring can be undermined by a change to how the score itself is calculated. Canada's LMIA route carries a concrete, dated example of exactly this kind of change: a positive Labour Market Impact Assessment issued on an application received on or after 1 May 2024 is valid for a maximum of 6 months, down from the previous 12 — a real change to a real number, not a hypothetical. This site's guide records assuming the eligibility rules are stable as its own pitfall for the same route.

Germany's self-employment residence permit adds a subtler version: assuming the settlement-permit timeline that follows it is fixed at a figure that may itself be out of date, rather than confirming the current figure directly.

## What this means before trusting any figure on this site, or anywhere else

Every specific figure quoted across these five routes, a fee, a qualifying period, a validity window, a capital threshold, is dated and sourced on its own programme page for exactly this reason. Treat any number more than a few months old, from any source, as something to reconfirm rather than something to build a plan around directly — not because guidance is usually wrong, but because two of the routes on this site alone have already changed enough to strand an applicant working from the previous version.
