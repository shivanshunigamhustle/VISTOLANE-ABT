## A visa application with a private gatekeeper in front of it

Not every route on this site is a straightforward conversation between an applicant and a government department. On a meaningful number of them, someone else decides first: an employer who has to run recruitment before they can even file, an endorsing body that assesses a business case on commercial terms, a sponsor who has to clear their own eligibility bar, an institution that issues the document the whole application rests on. The government still makes the final decision, but it is often the second decision, not the first — and the first one runs on different rules, different timelines, and sometimes a different incentive structure entirely.

## When the employer is the one under obligation

Canada's Labour Market Impact Assessment route makes this explicit in its own name: the assessment belongs to the employer, not the worker, and one of the pitfalls recorded on this site's LMIA guide is treating it as something a worker can personally obtain. The employer also carries a real, checkable obligation before they can even apply — a defined recruitment process, including a minimum period of advertising through the Government of Canada's own Job Bank — and advertising that falls short of the requirement is recorded as its own separate pitfall. Neither of these is something the applicant controls directly, which makes an LMIA-based application only as strong as the employer's own compliance.

The UK's Skilled Worker visa runs on a related structure: a valid Certificate of Sponsorship from a licensed employer is a hard prerequisite, and applying without one is recorded as a pitfall in its own right. The certificate is the employer's document, issued through the employer's own sponsor licence, not something the applicant can produce independently.

## When a third party assesses the business, not the government

Two of the investor-intent routes on this site put a commercial gatekeeper ahead of the government application. The UK's Innovator Founder visa requires endorsement from a Home Office approved endorsing body — a commercial organisation, not a government office — before the visa application can even begin, and this site's guide records underestimating that body as the real gatekeeper as its own pitfall, alongside the risk of approaching a body that has since been removed from the approved list. Canada's Start-up Visa runs on a similar structure, requiring support from a designated organisation; the fundraising and relationship-building that support depends on is, in practice, the harder half of the route.

Germany's self-employment residence permit puts the gatekeeping function inside the government process itself rather than a separate private body, but the effect is similar: the business plan is assessed against Germany's own economic interest and the venture's expected impact, and treating that plan as a formality rather than a genuine assessment is recorded as a pitfall on its own.

## When a sponsor's own status decides whether the application can proceed at all

Family and study routes carry a version of the same structure, where someone else's status has to be right before an applicant's own case can be considered. Canada's spousal sponsorship route requires the sponsor themselves to clear an eligibility bar, and missing that bar is recorded as a distinct pitfall, separate from anything about the relationship itself. Germany's spouse and family reunion route depends on the sponsor holding a residence status that specifically permits sponsorship — not every residence status does, and assuming otherwise is recorded as its own pitfall. Vietnam's TT family route goes further still: the process is initiated by the Vietnamese sponsor's own filing, and treating the route as something a foreign partner can pursue independently is recorded as a pitfall in its own right.

On the study side, Canada's Designated Learning Institution and the provincial attestation letter that increasingly sits alongside it are both gatekeeping documents an applicant does not control directly — confusing the attestation letter with the institution's own acceptance letter is recorded as a pitfall. Vietnam's DH student route carries a related risk: underestimating how much the sponsoring institution itself is involved in processing the visa, not merely issuing paperwork that starts it.

## Why this changes how a plan should be built

The practical consequence is the same across every one of these routes: a strong personal case is necessary but not sufficient when someone else's decision sits in front of the government's. The employer's recruitment compliance, the endorsing body's commercial judgement, the sponsor's own eligibility, the institution's own process — all of these run on their own timeline and their own criteria, largely outside the applicant's direct control. Building a plan that only accounts for the visa application itself, and treats the gatekeeper's decision as a formality along the way, is one of the most consistent ways these routes go wrong across every country this site covers.
