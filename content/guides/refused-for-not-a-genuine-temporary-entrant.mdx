## The test changed name, and the change matters

Australia's student visa was, until recently, assessed against the Genuine Temporary Entrant requirement — whether an applicant intended to stay only temporarily. For applications lodged on or after 23 March 2024, that test was replaced by the Genuine Student requirement, a meaningfully different question: not whether the applicant intends to leave, but whether their primary purpose is genuinely to study, and whether their chosen course fits their academic and career goals. An applicant, or an adviser, still preparing a statement to satisfy the old temporary-intent test is answering a question the assessor is no longer asking. This is the first thing worth confirming after a refusal on these grounds: which version of the test the application was actually assessed against.

## What actually persuades, on either version of the test

Under either the old or the new framing, refusals on this ground are reported as turning on the same underlying failure: a genuine circumstance, stated unpersuasively. A weak statement is reported as the most frequent cause of these specific refusals — not a false one, a thin one, where the applicant's real intentions did not raise concern on their own, but the written statement did not address the specific circumstances an assessor would weigh. A change in personal circumstances since a previous visa, a study choice that does not obviously build on a prior qualification, strong ties that were not clearly documented — all of these can produce a refusal on genuine-intent grounds even where nothing about the applicant's actual situation was in question.

## The same underlying test, differently named, elsewhere

This is not only an Australian pattern. The UK's Standard Visitor visa and Australia's own Visitor visa both carry a parallel requirement — genuine visitor or temporary intent — assessed the same way, on the specific evidence submitted rather than assumed from the stated purpose of the trip. Canada's visitor and eTA route carries a related risk from a different angle: this site's guide records treating an application as an entry guarantee even once granted, a reminder that genuine-intent scrutiny is not confined to the original application and can resurface at the border.

## What to actually do after a refusal on these grounds

Confirm first which specific version of the requirement the application was assessed against, since Australia's change in March 2024 means older guidance, including some professional advice not yet updated, may still describe a test that no longer applies in that form. Then treat the refusal as a signal about the statement, not the underlying facts — the circumstances that were true before the refusal are very likely still true, and the task on reapplication is building a more specific, more evidenced statement addressing exactly the concerns a refusal letter raises, not simply resubmitting the same case with different phrasing. A generic statement of genuine intent, however honest, is treated the same way on a second attempt as it was on the first.
