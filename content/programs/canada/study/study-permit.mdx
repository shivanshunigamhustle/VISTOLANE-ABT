## What the permit is, and what it is attached to

A study permit authorises someone to be in Canada for the purpose of study at a particular institution. It is not a visa and not a general permission to live in the country; it is permission bound to a course, an institution and a period. The institution matters more than applicants usually expect, because designation status governs not only whether the permit can be issued but what the qualification is worth on the other side.

Since 2024 the route has carried a second gatekeeper. Alongside the institution's decision to admit, most new post-secondary applicants need the province or territory to confirm that they hold one of a limited number of allocated places. Two separate bodies now have to say yes before an application is even complete, and they say yes to different questions.

## Who this suits, and who it does not

It suits someone who has chosen a programme deliberately, can fund the first year without relying on work in Canada, and has time for a sequence that runs through an institution, a province and a federal department in that order.

It suits less well anyone treating study as the cheapest available entry route. The financial threshold has to be evidenced before arrival, the work allowance during study is capped at a level that will not cover living costs, and the entitlements that once made a short course an attractive stepping stone have been narrowed. A person whose real objective is to work or to settle should compare this route honestly against applying for permanent residence directly, rather than assuming a course is the easier first step.

## How it connects to the rest of the system

Study is the entry point to Canada's most-travelled path to permanent residence: study, then work after graduation, then apply from inside the country with Canadian education and Canadian experience already counted. That sequence is real and it works for large numbers of people. What has changed is that each stage now has conditions that did not exist a few years ago, and satisfying the first no longer implies qualifying for the next.

The practical consequence is that the choice of programme is an immigration decision, not only an educational one. Two courses at the same price and the same length can lead to materially different outcomes afterwards depending on the institution's status and the field of study. Anyone whose plan depends on what happens after graduation should confirm the current rules before enrolling, not after.

## What applicants most often get wrong

The commonest error is financial and mechanical: assembling evidence against a threshold that has since moved. The requirement is revised every September, and it is a minimum to demonstrate rather than a forecast of what a year actually costs. Showing exactly the threshold, in a form that is hard to verify, and no more, is a weak application even when it is technically compliant.

The second is treating the acceptance letter as the substance of the application. It is necessary and it is not sufficient. The provincial attestation, where required, is a separate document from a separate authority, and applications are refused as incomplete without it.

A fourth is quieter and shows up later: choosing an institution on price alone. Designation status is a binary that sits underneath everything else, and it is not always obvious from a prospectus or an agent's description. A student can be genuinely accepted, genuinely enrolled, and genuinely paying, and still hold a qualification that does not carry the entitlements they were counting on. Checking the institution against the official list, personally, before any money moves, costs nothing and removes the single most expensive mistake available on this route.

The third is misreading the work allowance. Working off campus during study is permitted within a weekly cap and only while the conditions attached to it hold. It is not a route to funding a degree, and exceeding the limit is a status problem rather than an employment one.

## What happens after approval

An approval issued abroad is a decision, not the document. The permit itself is normally issued on arrival, and the officer at the port of entry makes the final call on admission — which is why travelling with the acceptance letter, the attestation and the financial evidence still matters after a positive decision.

Once studying, the permit's conditions are live obligations: remaining enrolled, making progress, and staying within the terms of any work allowance. Falling out of full-time study does not simply pause the arrangement, and restoring status afterwards is a harder application than maintaining it. The 90 days that follow the end of a programme exist to allow either an orderly departure or a further application, and are not a grace period for deciding what to do next.
