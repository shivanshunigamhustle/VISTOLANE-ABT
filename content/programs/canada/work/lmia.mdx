## What an LMIA actually is

A Labour Market Impact Assessment is a decision about a job, not about a person. Before certain Canadian employers may hire a foreign national, they have to ask Employment and Social Development Canada to look at one specific position — the duties, the wage, the hours, the work location — and judge whether filling it from abroad would have a positive, neutral or negative effect on the Canadian labour market. A positive assessment records that the department was satisfied on that question. It says nothing about the worker's admissibility, their qualifications, or their right to enter the country.

Almost every misunderstanding about this route traces back to that one fact. The assessment is attached to the job. It belongs to the employer, who applies for it, pays for it, and carries the obligations that come with it. The worker is named in the application but is not the applicant, and the worker's own application — the work permit — is a separate matter decided later by a different department, on different grounds.

That division of labour also explains why this route is slow in a way that is easy to underestimate. Two departments assess two applications in sequence, and before either of them begins, the employer has to spend weeks advertising a job they already know who they want to fill. None of that preparatory period appears in any published processing time, which is why a plan built on the headline figures alone will consistently run short.

## Who this route suits, and who it does not

This route suits a worker who already has a concrete offer from a specific employer, for a specific role, and where that employer is prepared to spend real money and several months on the hiring. It suits employers who can document a genuine recruitment effort and who are not simply looking for a cheaper hire, because the process is explicitly designed to test that.

It does not suit someone looking for work from abroad. There is no way to obtain an assessment speculatively, hold one, or shop it around; it does not exist independently of an employer and a position. Nor does it suit a worker whose prospective role sits in a low-wage occupation in a city where the labour market measure has closed the door, or an employer at a work location already at the cap for low-wage temporary foreign workers.

There is a second group this route serves badly: workers whose offer is real but whose employer is small. The obligations fall entirely on the business — the advertising, the record-keeping, the fee, the transition plan where one is required, and the exposure to a later inspection. A five-person company enthusiastic about a candidate in principle may lose that enthusiasm on contact with the paperwork, and a worker who has already given notice elsewhere is then stranded. Establishing early whether the employer has done this before, or has advice available, is worth more than any assessment of the candidate's own eligibility.

It is also the wrong route for anyone who is already eligible for a work permit that does not require an assessment at all. A significant volume of Canadian work permits is issued under exemptions, and an applicant who qualifies for one of those will reach the same outcome faster and more cheaply. Establishing whether an exemption applies is the single most valuable thing to do before starting down this path.

## How it sits next to Canada's other routes

Canada separates temporary status from permanent status, and this route is firmly on the temporary side. A work permit obtained this way is normally tied to the employer named in the assessment, which means the permission to be in Canada and the job are bound together. Leaving that employer is not simply a career decision.

Its real strategic value, for many people, is what it enables afterwards. Canadian work experience feeds into the permanent residence system, and a period of skilled work in Canada can change someone's standing considerably. That makes this route a step rather than a destination for a large share of the people who use it. It also means the sequence matters: the same person may reach permanent residence faster by studying first, working after graduation, and applying later, than by pursuing an employer-sponsored permit at the outset.

The comparison worth drawing is with the routes that skip the employer entirely. Permanent residence through the federal economic system is applied for directly by the individual, on the strength of their own age, education, language ability and experience, with no employer required to spend anything or wait for anyone. Where a candidate scores well on those measures, an employer-sponsored temporary permit may be a detour rather than a shortcut. Where they do not — because their occupation, age or language scores work against them — a Canadian job offer and the experience that follows it may be the only realistic way to move the numbers.

What that step is worth in points, however, is exactly the sort of detail that has changed in recent years, and this page does not state it. Anyone planning several years of their life around the answer needs it confirmed against the current rules rather than inferred from an older article.

## What people most often get wrong

The most damaging misunderstanding is commercial. Because a positive assessment is valuable and scarce, a market has grown around selling them, and the phrase turns up in advertisements aimed at workers overseas. There is nothing to buy. An employer who charges or recovers recruitment costs from the worker, directly or through anyone acting on their behalf, receives a negative decision — the rule exists precisely because this abuse is common. A worker asked to pay for one is being defrauded, exploited, or both.

The second is procedural, and it defeats employers rather than workers. Recruitment obligations are unforgiving: a set number of activities, run for a set duration, inside a set window before the application is filed, with evidence kept and every Canadian and permanent resident applicant accounted for, including the reason each was not hired. An advertisement that ran for three weeks instead of four, or that finished four months before submission, cannot be repaired retroactively. The employer advertises again and waits again.

The third is about timing after success. A positive decision starts a countdown, and the work permit application has to be made before it runs out. Employers who treat the positive letter as the finish line rather than the starting gun lose both the fee and, usually, the recruitment work behind it.

The fourth is an assumption of stability. The caps on low-wage hiring, the measure that refuses processing in higher-unemployment cities, and the wage thresholds that decide which stream applies have all moved more than once since 2024, sometimes at short notice. Guidance written eighteen months ago may describe a system that no longer exists.

## What happens after a positive decision

The employer receives the assessment letter and provides the worker with what they need to make their own application. The worker then applies for a work permit, pays their own fee, and in most cases gives biometrics. Approval abroad does not end the process either: the permit itself is normally issued at the port of entry, and a border services officer makes the final decision on admission.

That last point catches people out. An approval letter issued overseas is a decision to authorise, not the document itself, and the officer at the border retains the authority to ask questions and to refuse entry. Arriving with the job offer, the assessment reference and proof that the circumstances still match what was assessed is not excessive caution; it is the ordinary expectation.

Once working, the conditions on the permit govern the arrangement — the employer, the occupation and the location are fixed on its face. The employer's obligations do not end at hiring either; they remain subject to inspection on whether the wage, duties and conditions actually delivered match what was assessed. For the worker, the practical consequence is that the permit's value depends on a relationship with one employer, which is worth understanding clearly before relocating a family around it.
