## What Express Entry is

Express Entry is not an immigration programme. It is the machinery that manages applications for three of them — the Federal Skilled Worker Program, the Canadian Experience Class and the Federal Skilled Trades Program — and it works in two stages that are easy to collapse into one.

The first stage decides whether someone may enter the pool: they must meet the published minimum requirements of at least one of the three programmes. The second decides who is invited out of it: everyone in the pool is scored and ranked against everyone else, and invitations go to the top of the list. Meeting the requirements in full, with every box ticked, produces nothing on its own. Candidates who understand this plan differently from those who do not.

## Who this suits, and who it does not

It suits people whose case rests on their own attributes rather than on an employer's willingness to sponsor them: strong language ability, education, and skilled experience, ideally some of it Canadian. It rewards being young, well-qualified and fluent, and it does so mechanically, through a score.

It suits less well those whose profile is strong in ways the score does not measure. A candidate with two decades of senior experience in a field Canada needs may rank below a graduate a dozen years younger, because the ranking is not a judgement of professional worth. For those candidates the better route often runs through a provincial nomination, a Canadian job offer, or a period of Canadian study or work that changes the inputs — which is why this page should be read next to the temporary routes rather than instead of them.

It is also worth being blunt about the arithmetic: no amount of preparation converts a low score into an invitation. Someone whose score sits well below recent cut-offs and who has no realistic way to raise it is better served by an honest assessment early than by a profile sitting in the pool for a year.

## How it connects to the rest of the system

The most common path to permanent residence in Canada does not start here. It starts with a study permit or a work permit, and arrives at Express Entry two or three years later with Canadian education and Canadian skilled experience already earned — the exact inputs the Canadian Experience Class and the ranking system reward. Read that way, the temporary routes are not detours from permanent residence; for many people they are the mechanism.

The corollary is that decisions made years earlier constrain what is possible here. Which institution someone studied at, whether their post-graduation work was skilled, whether their permit allowed the work they actually did — all of it lands in this application eventually. That is an argument for looking at the whole sequence before committing to the first step of it.

## What applicants most often get wrong

The first error is treating eligibility as the finish line, and it produces the most disappointment: a profile that satisfies every published requirement, sitting in the pool, invited to nothing.

The second is sequencing. Language results and, where required, an assessment of foreign education must exist before a profile can be scored at all. Both take time that no published processing standard covers, and both are frequently left until after a candidate has decided to apply. Language is also the one factor most people can still move, and moving it is usually cheaper and faster than any other route to a higher score.

The third is building a plan on a scoring system that is being reconsidered. The federal high-skilled programmes and the ranking system have been through public consultation on reform, and individual scoring elements have changed before, sometimes materially and with little notice. A strategy that depends on how one factor is counted needs re-checking at the point of decision, not at the point the plan was made.

## What happens after approval

An invitation opens a limited window in which to file the full application. From receipt of a complete application, the published standard is six months to a decision for four cases in five — a genuine service standard, but one that measures only the final leg. The waiting that precedes it, in the pool, is the part nobody can forecast.

Approval grants permanent residence, which does not expire in the way a permit does. It carries obligations rather than an end date: a residency requirement to maintain the status, and a card that evidences it and must be renewed on its own schedule. Those two things are frequently confused, and the distinction matters when someone plans to spend significant time outside Canada after landing.
