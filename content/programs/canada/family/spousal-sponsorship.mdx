## What this route actually is

Spousal and partner sponsorship is not a points-based or employer-driven route like the other programmes on this site, it runs on relationship evidence and sponsor eligibility instead. A Canadian citizen or permanent resident sponsors their spouse, common-law partner or conjugal partner, and the two applications, the sponsorship and the sponsored partner's permanent residence, are submitted and assessed together as one package.

That structure means two things have to hold at once for this to succeed: the sponsor has to actually be eligible to sponsor, and the relationship has to be judged genuine on the evidence submitted. Either one failing sinks the application, independent of the other.

## Who this suits, and who it does not

It suits a couple who can document a real, lived relationship; shared finances, shared accommodation where they have lived together, a documented history of contact where they have spent time apart. It suits it less well a couple who have been together only briefly, or who have limited paper trail of the relationship, since the evidence burden here is real even where the relationship itself is not in doubt.

It is also worth being clear-eyed about the eligibility bars on the sponsor's side. A sponsor who is still under an earlier sponsorship undertaking, or who has certain unresolved immigration matters, is not eligible to sponsor regardless of how strong the relationship case is; this should be checked first, not discovered partway through assembling the application.

## Inland versus outland: a real choice, not a formality

Where the sponsored partner applies from affects both the processing timeline and, depending on current policy, whether they can work in Canada while the application is pending. This is genuinely one of the more consequential early decisions in this process, and it is marked unverified in this record deliberately; the specific trade-offs and current rules should be confirmed rather than assumed, because they are the kind of detail that shifts a real applicant's plan.

## Where this page is incomplete

Fee figures in particular are marked unverified here rather than stated as settled: different secondary sources currently quote different totals for the combined sponsorship, processing and Right of Permanent Residence fees, which is itself a signal that the fee schedule has moved recently. The current inland-versus-outland trade-offs, the minimum period that establishes a common-law relationship, and whether a sponsored partner can currently obtain an open work permit while waiting are all flagged the same way. None of these should be treated as settled without checking IRCC's own current guidance.
