## What this route actually is

The Start-up Visa is Canada's route for entrepreneurs, and it works differently from every other programme on this site: the hardest gate is not a government requirement at all, but a business one. Before IRCC assesses anything, the applicant has to convince a designated Canadian venture capital fund, angel investor group or business incubator to formally commit to the business. Only once that commitment exists does the immigration process, language testing, settlement funds, the permanent residence application itself, really begin.

That ordering is the single most important thing to understand about this route. Everything downstream of the designated organization's commitment is comparatively standard immigration processing; everything up to that point is a real fundraising or acceptance process with no guaranteed outcome and no fixed timeline.

## Who this suits, and who it does not

It suits an entrepreneur with a genuinely fundable or incubator-worthy business, who is prepared for the commitment-seeking process to take real, unpredictable time before the immigration clock even starts. It suits it badly as a fallback for someone without a strong business case; a designated organization's commitment is not a service that can be purchased or guaranteed, and an applicant who has not secured one has no application to make.

It is also worth being honest about the timeline. Recently reported average processing has run to around 52 months at the immigration-processing stage alone; a figure well outside what most people expect from a residence route, and one this record deliberately does not treat as fixed given the programme's recent history of policy change.

## A programme in flux

Unlike the other routes on this site, the Start-up Visa has recently been subject to real policy uncertainty: including signals from IRCC about transitional deadlines for applicants holding a letter of support issued in 2025, and mention of a new pilot programme communicated for 2026. That is exactly the kind of instability that makes a static description risky, which is why this record's quota and processing-time fields are both marked for verification rather than stated as settled; anyone relying on this page should confirm the programme's current intake status directly with IRCC before treating anything here as current.

## Where this page is incomplete

Beyond the processing time and quota uncertainty already flagged, several other fields are marked unverified: the exact current settlement funds threshold (which is revised periodically and varies by family size), the minimum investment thresholds tied to each type of designated organization, and the current requirements around business operations and timing. None of these should be treated as settled without checking IRCC's current published guidance.
