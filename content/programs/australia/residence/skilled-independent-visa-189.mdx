## What this route actually is

The Skilled Independent visa is Australia's points-tested permanent residence route for skilled workers who do not need — and are not relying on — an employer, a state government or a family member to get them in. Eligibility runs entirely on the applicant's own occupation, qualifications, experience and English ability, scored against a points test, with a minimum threshold to even register interest and a separate, higher, genuinely competitive threshold that decides who is actually invited to apply.

That gap between the legal minimum and the real bar is the single most important thing to understand about this route. 65 points opens the door to the waiting room; it does not get anyone through it.

## Who this suits, and who it does not

It suits a skilled worker whose occupation sits on the relevant list, who can obtain a positive skills assessment, and whose age, English ability, qualifications and experience combine to a genuinely competitive score — recently, that has meant 80 points or more for most occupations, not the 65-point statutory floor. It suits it badly as a plan built around the minimum requirement, since an Expression of Interest sitting well below the competitive threshold for its occupation may simply never receive an invitation.

It also, unlike the Skills in Demand visa on this site, requires no employer at all — which is its main appeal for a worker without a specific Australian job offer, but also means there is no employer absorbing any part of the process. The applicant carries the entire case alone.

## The points test is the whole strategy

Because eligibility and priority both run through the same points test, the practical work of this route is largely about maximising a genuine, evidenced score before ever submitting an Expression of Interest — age, English test result, skilled employment history, qualifications and, where applicable, a partner's skills all contribute. A well-evidenced application at a strong score moves through the invitation queue meaningfully faster than a marginal one, which is why the skills assessment and points-test evidence should be treated as the real front-loaded work of this route, not paperwork to finalise once an invitation arrives.
