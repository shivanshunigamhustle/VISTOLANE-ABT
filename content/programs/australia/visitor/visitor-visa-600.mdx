## Three routes, one intent

Australia does not have a single visitor visa; it has three, and which one applies to a given traveller depends entirely on their passport. The ETA and eVisitor are fast, low-cost or free options available only to holders of certain passports. Everyone else applies for the subclass 600 visitor visa itself, a longer and more document-heavy process that this record focuses on.

That split is worth checking before anything else. An applicant who is actually eligible for the ETA or eVisitor and applies for a subclass 600 instead is paying more and waiting longer for no benefit.

## Who this suits, and who it does not

The subclass 600 suits a genuine tourist or family visitor whose passport does not qualify for the faster options, and who can put together clear evidence of funds and of an intention to leave at the end of the stay. It does not suit anyone hoping to work while visiting; this route carries no work rights, and using it that way is a real breach, not a technicality.

Within the subclass 600 itself, the sponsored Family stream has been reported running notably slower than the standard Tourist and Business streams, worth factoring into any travel planning tied to a fixed date, such as a family event.

## What "genuine temporary entrant" actually means in practice

This requirement sits behind most of the document list on this route. It is not a single checkbox; it is assessed from the whole picture an application presents: financial capacity, ties to the home country, and how clearly the stated purpose of the trip lines up with the evidence submitted. Two applications with an identical stated purpose can be assessed differently if one presents materially thinner supporting evidence.
