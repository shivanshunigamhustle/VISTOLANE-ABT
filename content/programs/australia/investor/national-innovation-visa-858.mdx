## The honest starting point: the old investor route is closed

Australia's traditional capital-investment immigration route, the Business Innovation and Investment Program, subclasses 188 and 132, closed to new applications on 31 July 2024, as part of a wider migration strategy reform. A large amount of content describing that programme is still published and indexed online, and reads as current when it is not. Anyone researching an "Australia investor visa" today is likely to encounter it, and should treat anything that does not explicitly reference the National Innovation visa and its December 2024 introduction as potentially describing a route that no longer accepts applicants.

## What replaced it is not really an investor visa

The National Innovation visa (subclass 858) sits in this site's Invest intent because it is the closest current equivalent, but its actual test is achievement, not capital. Eligibility runs on an internationally recognised record of exceptional achievement in a priority sector of the Australian economy, assessed alongside the applicant's capacity to earn at a high income threshold. The Department is explicit that investment funds alone do not establish eligibility; a wealthy applicant without a genuinely exceptional, internationally recognised track record is not well positioned under this framework, regardless of how much capital they are prepared to bring.

## Who this suits, and who it does not

It suits an applicant who genuinely has an internationally recognised achievement record in a field that maps onto Australia's current priority sectors, and who can also evidence high income-earning capacity. It does not suit an applicant whose case is built primarily around available capital rather than personal achievement, that applicant no longer has an Australian route that fits, at least not one this site can honestly point to as of this record's review date.

## Where this page is incomplete

Because this is a comparatively new visa category, introduced 7 December 2024, and itself a replacement for a previous visa that also carried the subclass 858 number, reliable published data on fees, processing times and quota arrangements was limited at the time this record was written. All three are marked unverified here rather than guessed at. The current priority sector list and the specific evidentiary bar for "exceptional achievement" should also be confirmed directly with the Department of Home Affairs before advising a specific applicant.
