## What this route actually is

The Skills in Demand visa is Australia's main employer-sponsored temporary work visa, and its Core Skills stream is the one used most often. Structurally it mirrors the shape of Canada's employer-sponsored route on this site: an Australian business has to be approved to sponsor workers, that business nominates a specific position, and only then does the named worker apply for their own visa. Three separate approvals, in sequence, not one application.

That sequence matters for the same reason it matters in Canada. A worker cannot obtain sponsor status or a nomination on their own, both belong to the employer, and a plan built only around "get a job offer" skips two steps that have their own requirements and their own timing.

## Who this suits, and who it does not

It suits a worker who already has a concrete offer from an Australian business, in an occupation that genuinely sits on the eligible skilled occupation list, with at least a year of relevant recent experience to show for it. It suits employers who are established enough to hold or obtain Standard Business Sponsor approval and who are prepared to nominate a real, ongoing position.

It suits it badly if the occupation is not on the current eligible list, however senior or well-paid the role is; eligibility here runs through the occupation, not the salary alone. It also suits it badly if the intended employer has never sponsored before and has not yet begun that process; a job offer from a business that is not an approved sponsor is not yet a basis for this visa.

## What tends to catch people out

The most common gap is between having a job offer and having a nominated, sponsor-approved position; the two are not the same thing, and only the second is what this visa is actually built on. The second is occupation eligibility: a role can be genuinely skilled and still fall outside the current list, and that list is reviewed and changed, so a list checked a year ago is not evidence of eligibility today. The third is licensing: several of the occupations this visa covers are regulated in Australia, and an applicant with strong overseas credentials in a regulated field still has to clear the Australian licensing bar before the qualification counts for anything here.
