## What the Blue Card actually is

The EU Blue Card is Germany's main route for degree-qualified skilled workers with a job offer above a set salary line. Unlike an employer-sponsored route built around recruitment obligations, the German system leans on two comparatively simple tests: is the qualification real and recognised, and does the offer pay enough. There is no equivalent here to the weeks of mandatory advertising a Canadian employer has to run before applying for a Labour Market Impact Assessment — the German system instead gates on the salary threshold itself doing that filtering work.

## Who this suits, and who it does not

It suits someone with a genuine, recognised degree and a real job offer that clears the current salary threshold for their field. It suits it particularly well in designated shortage professions, where a lower threshold applies and the qualification bar is somewhat more forgiving. It suits it badly if the qualification cannot be shown to be comparable to a German one and the applicant does not work in the narrow field, IT, where a non-degree pathway currently exists — assuming that exception extends more broadly than it does is a common and avoidable mistake.

## Why the salary threshold is the whole game

Almost every other requirement on this route is downstream of the salary test. A job offer that clears the threshold, in a role matching a recognised qualification, is most of what is needed. A job offer that falls short of it does not become eligible no matter how senior the title or how strong the underlying qualification — and because the threshold is revised, most commonly on an annual basis, a plan built on last year's number is not a safe plan this year.

## How it compares to routes elsewhere

Set beside Canada's employer-sponsored route on this site, the EU Blue Card puts far less procedural weight on the employer and far more on a single, checkable number. That makes it comparatively fast to assess from the outside — a candidate can often tell within minutes whether an offer clears the bar — but it also means the one number that matters is exactly the number most likely to have moved since an article, including this one, was last checked.

## Where this page is incomplete

This is a new record. Several fields are deliberately left marked unverified: the residence permit application fee, processing times, whether the current shortage-profession list still gives IT specialists a genuine non-degree route, family reunification rules, and the pathway from the Blue Card to permanent settlement. The one figure this page does commit to — the salary threshold — is also the figure most worth re-checking on the day it is relied upon, because it changes on a predictable annual cycle rather than an occasional one.
