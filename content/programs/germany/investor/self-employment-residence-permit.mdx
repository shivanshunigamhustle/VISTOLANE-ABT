## What this route actually is

Germany's route for a founder or investor does not run on a capital threshold the way some countries' investor visas do; there is no fixed minimum amount of money that, on its own, qualifies an applicant. Instead, the residence permit for self-employment under Section 21 of the Residence Act is assessed on the business case itself: whether it serves a genuine economic interest or regional need, whether it is expected to benefit the German economy, and whether the applicant has secured financing genuinely sufficient for what the plan actually requires.

That structure changes what the real work of this application is. Where a fixed-threshold investor route is mostly about proving access to a set sum, this route is mostly about building and evidencing a credible business plan, which is a different, more open-ended kind of preparation.

## Who this suits, and who it does not

It suits a genuine founder or investor with a specific, well-developed business concept and the financing to match it, and the professional background to credibly execute it. It suits it less well anyone hoping that available capital alone, without a concrete business plan behind it, will carry the application; the absence of a fixed minimum is not an easier bar, it is a differently shaped one.

## A recent, favourable change worth knowing about

Holders of this permit have recently been reported able to apply for a settlement permit, permanent residence, after 3 years of successful business operation, down from a previous 5-year requirement. This is exactly the kind of detail that makes older guidance risky to rely on: content written before this change would understate how quickly this route can lead to permanent status.
