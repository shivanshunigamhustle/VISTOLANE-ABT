## What this route actually is

Family reunion to Germany for a spouse starts with a question that is easy to skip past: does the sponsoring spouse's current residence status even allow it. Not every residence permit in Germany carries family reunification rights — permanent residence and skilled-worker routes like the EU Blue Card are commonly reported as qualifying, but a sponsor on a more limited or temporary status may not be able to sponsor at all. That is worth confirming before anything else, since no amount of strong relationship or financial evidence fixes a sponsor who is not eligible to sponsor in the first place.

The second distinctive feature of this route, compared with family sponsorship elsewhere on this site, is the language requirement. Most non-EU spouse applicants have to demonstrate basic German — level A1 — before the visa is granted, not after arrival. That is a real, testable requirement most other countries' equivalent routes on this site do not carry in the same form.

## Who this suits, and who it does not

It suits a couple where the sponsoring spouse holds a qualifying residence status, and where the incoming spouse is prepared to reach basic German proficiency as part of the preparation rather than after moving. It suits it less well a couple assuming German language ability can be picked up casually after arrival — for most applicants on this route, it is a gate before entry, not a settling-in task.
