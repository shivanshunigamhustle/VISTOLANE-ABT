## What this route actually is

The Skilled Worker visa is the UK's main employer-sponsored work route, and it replaced the older Tier 2 (General) visa. Like Canada's employer-sponsored route and Australia's Skills in Demand visa on this site, it runs on a licensed-sponsor model: a UK employer has to hold Home Office approval to sponsor workers at all, and only then can it issue the document, a Certificate of Sponsorship, that a specific application is actually built on.

What sets this route apart from the others on this site is the salary mechanic. Rather than a single flat threshold, the requirement is the higher of a general minimum and an occupation-specific "going rate", which means the real bar moves depending on what the role actually is, not only on a flat number everyone is measured against.

## Who this suits, and who it does not

It suits a worker who already has a genuine offer from a UK employer that holds a sponsor licence, in a role whose specific going rate the salary actually clears. It suits it less well where the employer has never sponsored before, since obtaining a sponsor licence is its own process that has to happen before any individual Certificate of Sponsorship can be issued.

It is also worth being realistic about total cost before treating the headline visa fee as the whole picture. Between the application fee itself and the Immigration Health Surcharge, charged separately, per year of the visa's length, the all-in cost of a longer grant can be substantially higher than the visa fee alone suggests.

## The two numbers that decide most applications

Two figures do most of the work in whether an application succeeds: the skill level of the role, and the salary against it. Skill level and salary requirements have both been points of active policy change in recent years, including transitional and shortage-list arrangements that let some roles below the general skill threshold still qualify. That instability is exactly why this record marks both as needing verification rather than stating one fixed rule; an applicant working from an out-of-date threshold can end up structuring an entire job search around a number that has since moved.

## What happens once you are working

The visa is granted for up to 5 years before extension is required, which is a genuinely long runway compared with some other routes, and eligible applicants may become able to apply for indefinite leave to remain after that period. That said, the permission is tied to the sponsoring employer and the role on the Certificate of Sponsorship; changing employer or role while on this visa is not simply a career move, and generally requires a fresh sponsorship arrangement rather than continuing under the existing one.
