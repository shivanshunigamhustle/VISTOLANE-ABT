## What this route actually is

Indefinite Leave to Remain, known interchangeably as settlement, is the destination most other UK visa routes on this site are quietly built toward. It removes the expiry date, the tie to a specific purpose, and the work restrictions that come with a temporary permit, and it is generally the last step before a later, separate application for British citizenship becomes possible.

The qualifying period to reach it is not one fixed number. Most work and family routes point toward 5 years of continuous residence, but the general "long residence" route, for someone without a route-specific path, requires 10 years, and a proposed change reported as expected around autumn 2026 would lengthen the general period from 5 to 10 years. As of this record's last review, that change had not yet taken effect, which makes it one of the more consequential pieces of "watch this" information on the site.

## Who this suits, and who it does not

It suits anyone who has held a qualifying route for the required period, has genuinely tracked their absences against the 180-day rolling rule rather than assuming a rough travel pattern was fine, and has met the Life in the UK test and English language requirements well ahead of applying. It suits it less well anyone treating the qualifying period as a simple countdown with no active management; the 180-day absence rule is assessed on a rolling basis across the entire qualifying period, not year-by-year in isolation, and a pattern of travel that looked reasonable at the time can still fail that check.

## The fee is per person, not per family

This is worth stating plainly because it is easy to miscalculate: the reported £3,226 application fee applies to every individual named on the application, not once per household. A couple applying together, each with a qualifying route, pays that figure twice; a family of four pays it four times. Budgeting for settlement as a single fixed cost, rather than a per-person one, is one of the more common and expensive planning mistakes on this route.
