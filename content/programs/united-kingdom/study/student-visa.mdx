## What this route actually is

The UK Student visa is built around one document — the Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies, issued by a licensed sponsoring institution — but the requirement that trips up the most otherwise-eligible applicants is not the CAS itself, it is the 28-day maintenance rule. The applicant has to hold the required funds continuously for 28 days before applying, and the requirement is unforgiving: if the balance drops below the threshold on even a single day during that window, the 28-day clock resets from scratch.

That single mechanical rule is worth planning around more deliberately than almost anything else on this route. It rewards early, careful preparation and punishes last-minute fund transfers or accounts that dip briefly for unrelated reasons.

## Who this suits, and who it does not

It suits a genuine student with a confirmed offer who plans the maintenance funds requirement well ahead of applying, and who budgets for the real total cost — visa fee, course fees, and an Immigration Health Surcharge that is charged per year of the course, not as a flat one-off amount. A three or four-year degree multiplies that surcharge accordingly, which is easy to underestimate if only the headline visa fee is budgeted for.

It suits it less well anyone assembling funds reactively, close to the application date — between the CAS issuance timeline (reported at anywhere from about 5 working days to several weeks) and the mandatory 28-day maintenance window, a rushed application timeline on this route carries real, specific risk.
