## What this route actually is

Vietnam's Permanent Residence Card sits apart from every other route on this site's Vietnam coverage in one important way: it is not built around years of accumulated residence. Where many countries offer some form of "settle after enough time here" pathway, Vietnam restricts permanent residence to a limited set of specific statutory categories; close family ties to a Vietnamese citizen or permanent resident, certain foreign experts or scientists, and people recognized for meritorious service. Someone who has lived in Vietnam for a decade on renewed visas or a temporary residence card does not become eligible for permanent residence simply through that passage of time.

For the family-based category specifically, the reported requirement is at least 3 years of residence sponsored by a qualifying Vietnamese relative; considerably shorter than the years-long general residence routes seen elsewhere, but only available to someone who actually fits that specific category.

## Who this suits, and who it does not

It suits someone who genuinely falls within one of Vietnam's narrow statutory categories, most commonly close family ties to a Vietnamese citizen or permanent resident, and can document that clearly. It suits it less well a long-term foreign worker or investor assuming years of lawful residence in Vietnam will eventually convert into permanent status on its own; without fitting one of the specific categories, that conversion is not reported as available at all.

## Indefinite status, but not an indefinite card

Once granted, permanent residence itself does not expire. The physical Permanent Residence Card is a different matter; it is reported as requiring renewal every 10 years, a document-level formality rather than a re-assessment of the underlying right to remain. Treating the card the same way as the status it represents, and letting a renewal lapse, is an easy and avoidable mistake.
