## What this route actually is

The TT visa — from Thân nhân, Vietnamese for relatives — is the country's family-reunification route, most commonly used by the foreign spouse of a Vietnamese citizen. Its defining structural feature is that the process starts with the Vietnamese sponsor, not the foreign applicant: the sponsoring family member submits a sponsorship file to Vietnam's immigration authority, and only once that is accepted does the foreign family member's own visa application proceed.

The initial visa is reported valid for up to 12 months, with a follow-on temporary residence card — reported valid for up to 3 years — generally the next step for a family member planning to stay longer term.

## Who this suits, and who it does not

It suits a couple, or qualifying family relationship, with a cooperating Vietnamese sponsor able and willing to submit and support the sponsorship filing, and genuine documentation of the relationship (most commonly a marriage certificate). It suits it less well a foreign family member expecting to handle the process independently — this is not a route filed alone from abroad, and it also does not appear to carry general work rights, which anyone planning to work in Vietnam alongside joining family should check separately rather than assume.

## The residence card is the real long-term step

The TT visa itself is time-limited to a period reported at up to 12 months. For a family relationship intended to be long-term, the temporary residence card that generally follows — reported valid for up to 3 years — is the document that actually removes the need for repeated visa renewals. Planning toward that stage from the outset, rather than treating the initial visa as the whole process, gives a more realistic picture of what joining family in Vietnam actually involves.
