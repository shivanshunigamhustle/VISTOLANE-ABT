## What this route actually is

A Vietnamese work permit is the general authorisation a foreign national needs before taking up employment in Vietnam for more than 30 days, whether under a direct labour contract, an intra-company transfer, or several other working arrangements. Like the employer-sponsored routes elsewhere on this site, the process starts with the employer, not the worker: before anyone can apply for a permit, the employer has to formally report its need for foreign labour to the labour ministry or the relevant provincial authority.

That ordering is easy to underestimate. The employer's report has to be filed at least 30 days before the expected start date, and the worker's own application dossier then has to be submitted at least a further 15 days before work is due to begin. Both windows are minimums, not targets, and both sit in front of the actual 5-working-day decision the authority makes once a complete application is in hand.

## Who this suits, and who it does not

It suits a foreign worker who already has a confirmed role with an employer prepared to run the labour-need reporting process properly and in good time. It suits it less well anyone hoping to start work quickly and informally — the sequencing here does not compress well, and an employer unfamiliar with the process is likely to under-budget the lead time rather than the paperwork itself.

It is also worth checking, early rather than late, whether an exemption applies. Vietnam exempts several categories of foreign worker from needing a permit at all — short intra-company assignments under 90 days in a year, certain family circumstances, and sufficiently large capital contributions among them — and a worker who genuinely qualifies for one of these can save real time by confirming it before starting the full application process rather than after.

## What tends to go wrong

The most common failure is timing rather than eligibility: an employer that begins the process without the necessary lead time, or that submits the labour-need report and the permit dossier too close together rather than in the required sequence, ends up delaying a start date that was otherwise realistic. The second is documentation gathered from abroad — health certificates and criminal record certificates in particular often have their own validity windows and country-specific requirements, and assembling them under time pressure is where applications most often stall.
